Dr. John Langdon Haydon Down

A Man of Great Kindness and Empathy

Portrait of Dr. John Langdon Haydon Down from the National Library of Medicine, ca. 1870

A Timeline of Dr. John Down's Life

  • 1828 - Born November 18, 1828 in Cornwall England
  • 1843 - At age 14 he apprenticed at the Village Apothecary at Anthony St. Jacobs
  • 1846 - He had a chance encounter with a girl who presented with what would later become to be known as Down syndrome - inspired him to become a doctor
  • 1853 - Entered the Royal London Hospital as a student where he worked extracting teeth, washing supplies, and dispensing drugs
  • 1858 - Appointed as the Medical Superintendent at Earlswood Asylum in Surrey - he and his wife Mary transformed the Asylum from a place of horror to a happy place where punishment was prohibited and replaced with kindness, dignity, and respect
  • 1866 - Wrote a paper titled Observations on an Ethnic Classification of Idiots, which detailed the genetic condition now called Down syndrome, which was named after him
  • 1868 - Retired from Earlswood Asylum - he and his wife opened their private home to those with developmental and intellectual disabilities - they started with 18 residents and by 1876 had 160 residents
  • 1887 - Wrote the book titled Mental Affections of Childhood and Youth which detailed ideas and findings on Down syndrome, microcephaly, and savant syndromes
  • 1896 - Died as a respected member of his community with multiple awards, honors, gold medals, and accolades for all his contributions to the mentally disabled community
  • 1905 - his grandson was born and was diagnosed with Down syndrome
